Agentic Search is how Mira finds people, and it works differently from LinkedIn Recruiter and traditional keyword search. Describe who you're looking for in plain language, and Mira works like a seasoned recruiter.

---

## What it solves

Traditional candidate search has two built-in limits:

- **One database.** The right people are scattered across professional networks, code communities, research platforms, and company sites. A single database is bound to miss some.
- **One pass.** A single keyword match tends to return people with good-looking résumés, not people who are actually a good fit.

Agentic Search is different on both counts: it pulls from **many sources** and advances in **multiple steps**, deciding where to look next from what the last step surfaced, until it finds the right people, including passive candidates who aren't actively looking for a job.

## How it works

![After a broad request, Mira asks for details with a clarification form: the purpose of the search and who you're looking for](/docs/How-works.png)

1. **You describe the need**, in everyday language: the role, the must-haves, and the nice-to-haves.
2. **A Sourcing Agent searches** across many talent sources and the open web, following signals (companies, tech stacks, projects, public work) and cross-checking them.
3. **You get explainable results**: a candidate pool where every person comes with the reason they match.
4. **You refine.** If the direction is off, just say so or add a constraint, and Mira adjusts.

**Want a specific recruiting platform?** Name it. Say you want to search only that platform and Mira keeps the search there; mention it without "only" and Mira gives it priority. If a platform you asked for exclusively can't be reached, Mira asks before switching to another source instead of switching on its own. Platforms that need your sign-in are reached through the [Mira Browser Extension](/en/docs/tools-and-teamwork/mira-browser-extension).

## Core capabilities

- **Multi-source, multi-step**: matching records from public sources are merged and deduplicated, and each hop is cross-checked. Private contact records remain separate from public candidates, even when they refer to the same person. Powered by [MRE](/en/docs/match-quality-and-trust/understanding-ai-matching).
- **Evidence-weighed matching**: Mira evaluates each candidate against your must-haves and dealbreakers and ranks them by the evidence, surfacing the non-obvious (like the strong engineer who never updated their job title). Every candidate carries the reason behind the match.
- **On-demand contact**: once the right people are found, reveal email or phone as needed (each reveal uses credits).

To include your own talent library, ask Mira to search your saved or imported [Contacts](/en/docs/tools-and-teamwork/contacts), alongside public sources if needed. Your private contact library is available only in channels you own.

## Describe the role well

Mira understands natural language, but more context gives it more to reason about. A weak prompt and a strong one return very different candidate pools:

| Weak | Strong | Why it matters |
|------|--------|----------------|
| "Find me a developer" | "Senior backend engineer, 5+ years Python, has built microservices at scale, preferably from fintech" | More context means more signals to match against |
| "Marketing person in NYC" | "B2B SaaS marketing manager in NYC, has run product launches, comfortable with data-driven campaigns" | Concrete signals (launches, data-driven campaigns) give Mira something to check against, not just a title and a city |
| "We need someone good" | "Someone who's taken a product from 0 to 1, ideally at a Series A to B startup" | Mira reads "0 to 1" as early-stage building experience |

You can also lean on the implicit criteria you'd say out loud. Mira reasons about what they mean rather than matching them as keywords:

| You say | Mira reads it as |
|---------|------------------|
| "0 to 1" or "built from scratch" | early-stage company building, taking something from nothing to launch |
| "startup DNA" or "scrappy" | operates independently, comfortable with ambiguity and little structure |
| "high-growth background" | inferred from company size, funding history, and growth trajectory |
| "5+ years of experience" | read from the real career timeline, not a profile tag |
| "has built a team" | hired and led a team, not just worked on one |
